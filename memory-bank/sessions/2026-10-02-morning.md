## Physical questions and conclusions

### What state family is being discussed?
The attached paper is Freidel–Livine, *U(N) Coherent States for Loop Quantum Gravity*. Its fixed-area `U(N)` coherent states are Perelomov coherent states: the group `U(N)` acts on a highest-weight intertwiner. These are not a separate family from Perelomov states. The paper also relates them to fixed-spin Livine–Speziale coherent intertwiners; the `U(N)` state at fixed total area `J` is a weighted superposition over spin assignments with that sum.

### What is the highest-weight intertwiner?
With Schwinger oscillators `a_i,b_i` on leg `i`,
```
F†₁₂ = a†₁b†₂ − a†₂b†₁
```
creates an `SU(2)`-invariant pair across legs 1 and 2. The reference vector is
```
|J;12⟩ = (F†₁₂)^J |0⟩ / √(J!(J+1)!)
```
(up to the paper's spinor-label and normalization conventions). It is a two-leg singlet with spin `j₁=j₂=J/2`. Here `J` labels total area / the individual leg spins; the combined `SU(2)` angular momentum remains zero. Acting by represented `U(N)` generates the coherent family. `U(N)` is implemented by bilinears `Eᵢⱼ=a†ᵢaⱼ+b†ᵢbⱼ`, which preserve total boson number. This resembles a generalized group displacement, while oscillator displacement `D(α)=exp(αa†−ᾱa)` belongs to the Heisenberg–Weyl group and uses linear, number-changing generators. The state labels describe a group orbit modulo its stabilizer, rather than a one-to-one copy of all `U(N)` elements.

### How do Schwinger occupations become |j,m⟩?
On each leg,
```
J_z=(a†a−b†b)/2,  J_+=a†b,  J_−=b†a.
```
A normalized occupation state is already an angular-momentum eigenstate:
```
|n_a,n_b⟩ = (a†)^n_a (b†)^n_b |0⟩ / √(n_a! n_b!)
j=(n_a+n_b)/2,  m=(n_a−n_b)/2,  |n_a,n_b⟩=|j,m⟩.
```
This is a relabeling by the eigenvalues of `J²,J_z`, not a further physical transformation.

### What does F†₁₂ and its square create?
For one pair,
```
F†₁₂|0⟩ = |½,½⟩₁|½,−½⟩₂ − |½,−½⟩₁|½,½⟩₂.
```
Dividing by `√2` gives the normalized singlet.

For two pairs,
```
(F†₁₂)²|0⟩ = 2( |1,1⟩₁|1,−1⟩₂ − |1,0⟩₁|1,0⟩₂ + |1,−1⟩₁|1,1⟩₂ ).
```
The normalized state has the same parenthesized sum divided by `√3`. Each leg now carries spin 1 (the triplet representation), but the pair of legs is still in a total singlet. One product component, such as `|1,1⟩₁|1,−1⟩₂`, has `M=0` but is not itself a definite total `J`; it contains coupled `J=0,1,2` contributions. The full three-term combination cancels the `J=1,2` parts.

### Is the construction analogous to RVB?
Yes, as a singlet-bond analogy. `(F†ᵢⱼ)^J` repeats a bond between the same pair of legs, with bosonic symmetrization producing spin `J/2` at each end. A general `F†_z=Σᵢ<ⱼ zᵢⱼF†ᵢⱼ` raised to power `J` superposes bond placements. This is RVB-like, but it is not the same as a conventional lattice RVB dimer-covering state: the spaces and symmetrization constraints differ.
